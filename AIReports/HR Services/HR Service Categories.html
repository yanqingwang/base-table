1. Executive Summary 摘要

This report evaluates three ways to categorize HR services as a global "product" catalog, each structured in 2–3 layers (L1 / L2 / L3):

  • Scenario 1 — By traditional HR function (e.g., Compensation, Talent Acquisition): logical for HR, moderately intuitive for employees.
  • Scenario 2 — By HR org chart (e.g., CoE, HRBP, Shared Services): efficient for internal routing, but an anti-pattern for employee-facing navigation.
  • Scenario 3 — By HR SSC service model (employee lifecycle "moments that matter" + tiered delivery): most intuitive for employees and managers, best fit for a global shared services operating model.

Core Recommendation 核心建议

Adopt a "dual taxonomy" (front-stage / back-stage decoupling) 前后台解耦的双层分类体系:

  • Front stage (employee/manager-facing): Scenario 3 — lifecycle & persona-based categories ("Pay & Money", "My Career", "Manage My Team"), in the employee's language, not HR's language.
  • Back stage (fulfillment & routing): Scenario 1 — functional service domains mapped to assignment groups, with location / BU / employee-group routing attributes.
  • Never expose the HR org chart (Scenario 2) to employees. Use it only as a routing dimension that can change without breaking the employee experience. 员工不需要知道HR内部如何分工。
  • Search-first, categories as safety net: invest in search, synonyms and AI-assisted intake as the primary path; categories serve browsing and discoverability.

3. Scenario 1 — Taxonomy by Traditional HR Function 按传统HR职能分类

Logic: Categories mirror the classic HR functional disciplines. This is how HR professionals think, and how most HR process architectures (e.g., APQC "Develop and Manage Human Capital") and HCM systems are structured.

L1: 1. Talent Acquisition & Onboarding 招聘与入职 ├─ L2: Recruiting & Internal Mobility │ ├─ L3: Open a job requisition (manager) │ ├─ L3: Refer a candidate │ └─ L3: Apply for internal transfer ├─ L2: Pre-boarding & New Hire │ ├─ L3: Background check status inquiry │ ├─ L3: New hire document submission │ └─ L3: IT/workspace provisioning request (manager) └─ L2: Onboarding & Probation ├─ L3: Onboarding checklist support └─ L3: Probation review submission (manager) L1: 2. Compensation & Benefits 薪酬与福利 ├─ L2: Compensation │ ├─ L3: Salary review / off-cycle adjustment (manager) │ ├─ L3: Bonus / incentive inquiry │ └─ L3: Compensation statement request ├─ L2: Benefits │ ├─ L3: Benefits enrollment / life-event change │ ├─ L3: Insurance claim support │ └─ L3: Country allowance request // localized L3 └─ L2: Recognition & Equity ├─ L3: Recognition award nomination (manager) └─ L3: Stock/equity plan inquiry L1: 3. Payroll, Time & Absence 工资、考勤与休假 ├─ L2: Payroll │ ├─ L3: Payslip inquiry / discrepancy │ ├─ L3: Tax form / certificate request │ └─ L3: Bank account change ├─ L2: Time & Attendance │ ├─ L3: Timesheet correction │ └─ L3: Overtime / shift-swap request // manufacturing-critical └─ L2: Absence & Leave ├─ L3: Annual leave inquiry ├─ L3: Parental / maternity leave case └─ L3: Sick leave & medical certificate submission L1: 4. Learning & Development 学习与发展 ├─ L2: Training & Compliance Learning ├─ L2: Leadership & Career Development └─ L2: Education Assistance & Certification L1: 5. Performance & Talent Management 绩效与人才管理 ├─ L2: Goal Setting & Performance Reviews ├─ L2: Talent Review & Succession (manager/HR) └─ L2: Promotion & Job Change (manager) L1: 6. Employee Relations & Compliance 员工关系与合规 ├─ L2: Workplace Concerns & Grievances // confidential case handling ├─ L2: Disciplinary & Investigations (manager/HR) └─ L2: Policy Interpretation & Labor/Works Council L1: 7. HR Administration & Data 人事行政与数据 ├─ L2: Personal Data Changes (name, address, dependents) ├─ L2: Letters & Verifications (employment letter, visa letter) └─ L2: Contracts & Documents L1: 8. Global Mobility 全球流动 ├─ L2: International Assignment & Relocation └─ L2: Immigration & Work Permits L1: 9. Offboarding & Retirement 离职与退休 ├─ L2: Resignation & Exit Process ├─ L2: Retirement Planning └─ L2: Alumni & Rehire

✔ Strengths 优势

  • Maps 1:1 to HR process architecture and CoE ownership → simplest governance and clearest service-owner model.
  • Aligns with ServiceNow HRSD "COE" structure and most HCM module structures.
  • Stable — HR functions rarely change even when the org chart does.
  • Easy process-to-service mapping (SC-007).

✘ Weaknesses 劣势

  • Employee must know which function owns their problem. "I'm getting married — is that Benefits, Payroll, Leave, or HR Admin?" (Answer: all four.)
  • Cross-functional life events (relocation, new baby, promotion) fragment across categories.
  • Category names risk HR jargon ("Total Rewards", "Workforce Administration").
  • Manager tasks are scattered rather than consolidated.

4. Scenario 2 — Taxonomy by HR Org Chart 按HR组织架构分类

Logic: Categories mirror how the HR organization is actually structured (Ulrich-style three-pillar model): CoEs, HRBPs, and HR Shared Services, plus local HR teams.

L1: 1. HR Shared Services Center (HRSSC) 共享服务中心 ├─ L2: Tier-1 Contact Center 一线服务台 │ ├─ L3: General HR inquiry │ └─ L3: Portal / system navigation help ├─ L2: Payroll Operations Team │ ├─ L3: Payslip discrepancy case │ └─ L3: Tax certificate issuance ├─ L2: Workforce Administration Team │ ├─ L3: Data change processing │ └─ L3: Employment letter issuance └─ L2: Onboarding/Offboarding Operations Team L1: 2. Centers of Expertise (CoE) 专业中心 ├─ L2: Total Rewards CoE │ ├─ L3: Compensation benchmarking request (HR/manager) │ └─ L3: Benefits plan design escalation ├─ L2: Talent Acquisition CoE ├─ L2: Talent Management & L&D CoE ├─ L2: Employee Relations CoE └─ L2: Global Mobility CoE L1: 3. HR Business Partners (HRBP) 业务伙伴 ├─ L2: BU-A HRBP Team │ ├─ L3: Org design consultation (manager) │ └─ L3: Workforce planning support (manager) ├─ L2: BU-B HRBP Team └─ L2: Regional HRBP Teams L1: 4. Local / Country HR 属地HR ├─ L2: Country HR Operations (statutory filings, local benefits) └─ L2: Site HR (plants/factories) (shift issues, works council) L1: 5. HR Technology & Analytics (HRIT) HR技术与分析 ├─ L2: HR Systems Support └─ L2: HR Reporting & Data Requests

⚠ Anti-Pattern Warning for Employee-Facing Use 员工端反模式警告

  • Conway's Law problem: the catalog ships your org chart. Employees should never need to know whether Payroll sits in the SSC or in Finance, or which CoE owns benefits.
  • Fragile: every HR reorg breaks the navigation, retrains 50,000 employees, and invalidates bookmarks and muscle memory.
  • Inconsistent globally: the same service is delivered by SSC in Region A and local HR in Region B — an org-based taxonomy cannot present one consistent global catalog (directly conflicts with SC-017 / SC-020).

Legitimate use 合理用途: as the back-stage routing & assignment-group model (who fulfills), and for the HR-facing internal catalog (HR-to-HR requests, e.g., HRBP requesting CoE benchmarking). It should exist as metadata behind every L3 item — never as front-stage navigation.

5. Scenario 3 — Taxonomy by HR SSC Service Model 按HR共享服务模式分类

Logic: Designed the way a shared services organization productizes services: front-stage categories follow the employee lifecycle and "moments that matter", expressed in the employee's own words; delivery is organized by service tiers (Tier 0 self-service → Tier 1 generalist → Tier 2 specialist → Tier 3 CoE/HRBP). This is the model most mature ServiceNow HRSD implementations converge on.

5.2 Back Stage — Tiered Delivery Model 后台分层交付模型

TierWho / WhatHandlesTarget Share
Tier 0Portal, knowledge base, AI chatbot / virtual agent, HCM self-servicePolicy questions, how-to, status checks, simple transactions60–75% deflection
Tier 1HR Service Center generalists (regional hubs, follow-the-sun, multilingual)Guided transactions, first-touch case resolution, triage20–30%
Tier 2SSC specialist teams (Payroll, Benefits, Mobility, Onboarding ops)Complex transactions, exceptions, vendor coordination5–10%
Tier 3CoE experts & HRBPsPolicy design, ER investigations, strategic consultation<5%
Tier 4External vendors, legal counsel, works councilsOutsourced fulfillment, legal escalationsException-based

Key principle: the tier is invisible to the employee. Every front-stage L3 item maps to a fulfillment path (which tier, which team, in which country) via routing metadata — this is where Scenario 1 (function) and Scenario 2 (org) live on as attributes.

✔ Strengths 优势

  • Highest employee intuitiveness — categories match how people describe their need ("I'm having a baby", not "concurrent benefits + statutory leave transactions").
  • Manager Hub consolidates all people-manager tasks in one place — top driver of manager satisfaction.
  • Life-event journeys orchestrate cross-functional bundles (SC-021).
  • Resilient to HR reorgs and operating-model variance across countries (SC-017).
  • Natural fit for tier-0 deflection, chatbots and SSC cost metrics.

✘ Weaknesses / Mitigations 劣势与缓解

  • Services can plausibly belong to 2 categories ("Parental leave": Time Off or Family Changes?) → mitigate with polyhierarchy (one canonical home + cross-links) and strong search.
  • Requires an explicit mapping layer to processes and owners → heavier initial design effort (addressed by SC-007/SC-018).
  • Journey bundles need workflow orchestration capability (parent case + child tasks).

Recommended Target Architecture 推荐目标架构 — "3 + 1 + org-as-metadata"

  1. Front stage = Scenario 3 (8 L1 lifecycle/persona categories) for the employee portal, chatbot topics and knowledge browsing.
  2. Service registry backbone = Scenario 1 (functional service domains) as the canonical service master: each L3 item has exactly one functional home, one global service owner, one process mapping. This satisfies SC-006/SC-013/SC-020.
  3. Org chart = routing metadata only (Scenario 2): assignment groups per country/BU/tier attached to each L3 item; changeable without touching navigation.
  4. One shared taxonomy spine (L1/L2 global-fixed, L3 governed-flexible) reused by the service catalog, knowledge base, chatbot intents, and reporting (SC-009/SC-011).

7. UX / IA / Navigation / Search 体验与信息架构

AreaQuestionFindings & Recommendations
UX How do employees search for HR services? Observed behavior across mature deployments follows a consistent pattern: employees describe symptoms and life situations, not HR service names — "my payslip is wrong", "having a baby", "证明信怎么开". Typical entry-path mix: search box first (~40–60%), direct links from email/chat notifications (~20%), category browsing (~15–25%), chatbot (growing). Frontline manufacturing workers browse tiles more and search less (typing on shared kiosks/mobile is high-friction) → large icon-based L1 tiles and voice/chat entry matter there. Action: instrument search logs and failed-search terms from day one; they are the cheapest continuous taxonomy-validation instrument.
IA What category names are intuitive for employees? Rules: (1) use employee vocabulary, not HR vocabulary — "Pay & Money", never "Total Rewards"; "Time Off", never "Absence Management"; (2) validate with card sorting (open, 30–50 participants per major region/persona) then tree testing (task-based, target ≥80% first-click success); (3) test translated labels separately — a name that works in English may fail in German or Chinese; (4) avoid internal program brand names ("Project Phoenix Portal") as category labels; (5) 6–9 L1 items, no more.
Navigation Service-first vs Process-first navigation? Service-first (outcome-first) wins for employees: they want the letter, the corrected payslip, the leave approved — not to "execute the leave management process". Process-first navigation is appropriate only for the HR-internal workspace (fulfillers think in processes). Hybrid tactic: expose journeys ("I'm relocating") that orchestrate multi-step processes behind a single service front door. Provide 3 parallel paths: search / L1 browse / persona quick-links (top 10 tasks by role and country — data-driven, refreshed quarterly).
Search Should search drive routing instead of categories? Yes — search (and AI intake) should be the primary router; categories are the browsable safety net and the SEO/discoverability layer. Requirements: multilingual synonym dictionaries per country; misspelling tolerance; results blending knowledge (Tier 0) above request forms (deflection-first ordering); "zero-results" alert workflow feeding taxonomy governance; AI/virtual-agent intent model trained on the same shared taxonomy (SC-009/SC-011) so chatbot, search and catalog never disagree. Categories remain necessary: ~15–25% of users browse, auditors/HR need a complete inventory, and new employees don't know what to search for.

8. Research Requirements — Detailed Responses 研究需求逐项响应 (SC-005 – SC-029)

8.1 Taxonomy Structure 分类结构 (SC-005 – SC-009)

IDTopicRecommended Approach
SC-005Define L1/L2/L3 service hierarchyAdopt the 3-layer model in §2.1: L1 = 8 lifecycle domains (front stage), L2 = 4–8 service groups per domain, L3 = requestable catalog items with owner/SLA/audience/country scoping. Hard limits: ≤9 L1, ≤8 L2 per L1; anything needing a 4th navigational level indicates an L3 that should be a guided form, not a category.
SC-006Enterprise service taxonomy modelFour-facet model: every L3 item carries (1) Service Domain (functional home, Scenario 1), (2) Audience/Persona (employee, manager, HR, contingent, alumni…), (3) Location scope (global / region / country list), (4) Delivery attributes (tier, channel, fulfiller group, SLA class). Front-stage category is a presentation mapping, not the master key. Master key = globally unique Service ID.
SC-007Process-to-service mapping frameworkMany-to-many matrix: Global HR Process Model (L1–L4, e.g., APQC-style) ↔ Service Catalog (L1–L3). Rules: every L3 service maps to ≥1 process; every customer-triggered process variant maps to exactly 1 canonical service; journeys (life events) map to process bundles. Maintain in the service registry, not in slideware.
SC-008Align catalog with global HR process architectureAnchor the functional backbone (Scenario 1) to the company's global HR process house (or APQC "Develop and Manage Human Capital" as reference if none exists). Alignment check runs at every taxonomy release: no orphan processes (customer-facing process without a service) and no orphan services (service without a documented process).
SC-009Knowledge taxonomy vs service taxonomyOne shared spine: knowledge articles use the same L1/L2 as services, extended with knowledge-specific facets (content type: policy / how-to / FAQ / form; validity country; language). Every L3 service links its supporting articles; every article links to its "escalation service" ("still need help? → open this request"). This powers deflection-first search ordering and consistent chatbot intents.

8.2 Taxonomy Governance 分类治理 (SC-010 – SC-012)

IDTopicRecommended Approach
SC-010Governance for taxonomy ownershipSingle accountable owner: Global HR Service Management Office (HR-SMO) owns L1/L2 and the taxonomy standard. A cross-functional Taxonomy Board (HR-SMO chair + CoE reps + regional SSC leads + HRIT + KM lead) approves L1/L2 changes; L3 changes follow a lightweight intake with HR-SMO sign-off. RACI published; no "shadow catalogs".
SC-011Is a shared taxonomy required?Yes — mandatory at L1/L2 across all countries, BUs, and channels (portal, chatbot, knowledge, telephony IVR, reporting). Without it: broken global reporting, duplicated knowledge, inconsistent chatbot intents, impossible cross-country benchmarking. Local flexibility is granted only at L3 (see SC-022–025).
SC-012Future taxonomy governance modelOperate taxonomy like a product: quarterly release cycle; change intake form (requester, driver, impact analysis, affected countries); data-driven review inputs (search zero-results, misrouted-case rate, catalog items with <5 requests/quarter → merge/retire candidates); annual card-sort/tree-test refresh; version-controlled taxonomy artifact with deprecation policy (retired categories redirect, never 404).

8.3 Ownership & Routing 所有权与路由 (SC-013 – SC-021)

IDTopicRecommended Approach
SC-013Service owner modelEvery L3 item has exactly one Global Service Owner (typically in the functional CoE or Global Process Owner org) accountable for: service definition, global template, SLA, KPIs, variance approvals, continuous improvement. Regional/country Service Delivery Owners own fulfillment performance locally. Owner recorded as structured data in the registry, not in documents.
SC-014Case owner vs task ownerCase Owner = single point of accountability for the end-to-end case and all employee communication; holds SLA clock; never "throws over the wall". Task Owners = fulfill discrete child tasks (e.g., IT provisioning inside onboarding case) with task-level SLAs; communicate to the case owner, not to the employee. Rule: the employee always has exactly one visible owner.
SC-015Routing frameworkDeterministic routing matrix keyed on: Service ID + employee's country + BU + employee group (staff/frontline/executive/contingent) + language + sensitivity flag → assignment group + tier. Precedence: (1) confidentiality overrides (ER, executive comp → restricted queues), (2) statutory country rules, (3) BU-specific rules, (4) default global route. Keep the matrix as configuration data with governance, never hard-coded.
SC-016Primary ownership rulesTie-breakers when a service could belong to two functions: (1) who owns the policy that governs the outcome; (2) who fulfills >50% of effort; (3) who owns the system of record for the resulting data; (4) escalation → Taxonomy Board decides, decision logged as precedent. One primary owner is mandatory; secondary teams are contributors via tasks.
SC-017Operating model variance handlingMaintain a Variance Register: same L3 service, per-country delivery attributes (fulfiller = SSC hub vs local HR vs vendor; channel; SLA). The employee-facing definition stays identical; only the fulfillment path varies. Variances are approved by the Global Service Owner, time-boxed where transitional (e.g., "local until payroll migration to hub in 2026").
SC-018Service decomposition methodologyDecompose by outcome, not by team. A candidate L3 passes if: (a) an employee/manager would request it as a standalone need; (b) it has one definable owner; (c) it has a measurable completion state; (d) its volume justifies a distinct form/workflow (else fold into a general inquiry with a category tag). If a "service" is only ever a step inside another service, it's a task, not an L3.
SC-019Process-to-task ownership modelFor each multi-team service, publish a step-level RACI: process step → task → owning team → SLA → hand-off trigger. Ownership follows the task, accountability stays with the case owner (SC-014). Review hand-off failure points quarterly using case reassignment-count metrics.
SC-020Eliminate duplicate service definitions(1) Canonical Service Registry with unique Service IDs as single source of truth; (2) naming convention: [Outcome verb] + [object] (+ audience), e.g., "Request employment verification letter"; (3) dedup audit at migration: cluster existing items by name similarity + fulfillment group + form fields, merge to canonical items with country scoping instead of country copies ("Employment letter – Germany" + "Employment letter – France" → 1 item, 2 country variants); (4) new-item intake includes mandatory duplicate check.
SC-021Multi-team workflow architectureParent-case + child-task orchestration pattern: life-event journeys (onboarding, relocation, exit) instantiate a parent case (single employee-visible thread) spawning parallel/sequential child tasks across HR functions, IT, Facilities, Finance, vendors. Design rules: dependencies modeled explicitly; child task failure alerts parent owner; employee status view aggregates child progress; cross-departmental tasks use enterprise (not HR-only) task types where other departments fulfill.

8.4 Global vs Local 全球与本地 (SC-022 – SC-025)

IDTopicRecommended Approach
SC-022Global vs local design frameworkThree-tier model: Global Core (L1/L2 names, service definitions, case model, KPIs — fixed), Regional Variant (delivery attributes, languages, hub assignment — governed flexibility), Local Mandatory (statutory L3 items existing only in one country, e.g., China housing fund 住房公积金, German works-council consultations, Brazil FGTS). Target ratio ~80% global / 20% local by item count; measure and report drift.
SC-023Country variance governanceVariance request classes: Class A – Statutory/legal (fast-track, evidence of legal basis required, auto-approved by Global Service Owner); Class B – Works council / union agreement (approved with agreement reference and expiry tracking); Class C – Local preference ("we've always done it this way" — requires business case, default answer is no). All variances logged in the Variance Register with review dates.
SC-024Global template strategyEvery L3 has a global template: canonical form, workflow, knowledge article skeleton, SLA class, KPI set. Countries inherit the template and apply approved variances as configuration layers (never forked copies). Templates version-controlled; releases pushed in scheduled trains (e.g., quarterly) with regression checks on localized layers; new-country onboarding = instantiate templates + local mandatory items, typical setup measured in weeks not months.
SC-025Localization methodologyFour localization layers, applied in order: (1) Language — labels, forms, knowledge, search synonyms (professional translation + in-country review); (2) Legal/statutory — mandatory fields, retention rules, consent, works-council visibility constraints, data-residency; (3) Cultural/channel — e.g., WeChat/DingTalk-adjacent habits in China, works-council communications in Germany, kiosk-first for plants; (4) Operational — local fulfiller mapping, hours, holiday calendars. Each layer has a checklist owned by the country HR lead and signed off by the Global Service Owner.

8.5 External Benchmarking 外部对标 (SC-026 – SC-029)

SC-026Benchmark ServiceNow HRSD service catalogsServiceNow HRSD ships a reference structure of COEs (typically: Workforce Administration, Payroll, Total Rewards / Benefits & Compensation, Employee Relations, Talent Management, HR IT/Systems, plus Lifecycle Events for journeys) with HR Services beneath them and Employee Center topics as the employee-facing navigation layer — i.e., the product itself embodies the dual-taxonomy pattern recommended in §6 (COE = back stage, Topics = front stage). SC-027Collect peer company taxonomy modelsConvergent pattern across large global implementations: 6–9 employee-facing L1 categories, lifecycle-flavored naming ("Pay", "Time Away", "Career", "Life Events", "Manager"), separate manager section, search-first portal. Collection method: (a) ServiceNow/vendor customer reference sessions, (b) Knowledge conference case studies, (c) peer networks (shared services forums), (d) screenshots/walkthroughs under NDA. Deliverable: side-by-side taxonomy comparison of ≥6 peers with L1 label frequency analysis.
SC-028Compare manufacturing company implementationsManufacturing-specific findings to validate: (1) deskless access is the #1 differentiator — kiosk/mobile/QR entry, badge-tap auth, large-tile UI; (2) supervisor proxy requests on behalf of line workers is a required capability; (3) shift/overtime/attendance services dominate volume (often >40% of cases at plants) → elevate "My Time" in navigation for plant personas; (4) works councils/unions constrain case visibility and require consultation steps in workflows; (5) multilingual plants need per-site language defaults. Benchmark 3–5 manufacturers of comparable footprint.
SC-029Compare routing architecture patternsPatterns observed: (a) geography-first (route to country team, then skill) — simple, but hub economics suffer; (b) skill/service-first with location as attribute — best for global hubs, recommended target; (c) tier-based with swivel escalation — universal baseline; (d) AI-assisted intake routing (classify free-text to service + route) — fastest-growing, requires the shared taxonomy (SC-011) as its label set; (e) follow-the-sun queues for 24/7 coverage. Recommendation: (b)+(c) now, pilot (d) on top of clean taxonomy.
