**Report Date: 27 April 2026**  
**Topic: Global Human Resources Delegation of Authority (DOA), approval governance, HR systems, compliance, and audit controls**  
**Language: English**

## Executive Summary

HR Delegation of Authority (HR DOA) is not merely an approval table. It is a governance and internal-control mechanism that connects who may make HR decisions, who remains accountable, who performs professional review, who can veto high-risk actions, and how the organisation preserves evidence. The internal problem file identifies HR DOA as covering recruitment, hiring, compensation, promotion, transfer, discipline, termination, restructuring, contingent labour, and employee-data access, while requiring clear answers on authority boundaries, accountability chains, system rights, exceptions, conflicts of interest, global consistency, and local compliance.[citation:1]

This report defines HR DOA as **a risk-tiered HR decision-rights system structured around the employee lifecycle, supported by segregation of duties, documented evidence, system controls, and auditability**. It should support governance, operational efficiency, reliable reporting, and legal compliance. COSO frames internal control as supporting operations, reporting, and compliance objectives; the IIA Three Lines Model clarifies the interaction among governance, risk management, and internal audit; ISO 37000, ISO 37301, and ISO 31000 provide the principles for organisational governance, compliance management, and risk management.[citation:2][citation:3][citation:4][citation:5][citation:6]

The recommended design is: **global principles, local legal priority, consistent risk language, and locally calibrated thresholds**. The UN delegation framework emphasises transparency, accountability, and decision-making closer to mandate and programme delivery, while the G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance emphasise disclosure, board responsibilities, and conflict-of-interest disclosure. HR DOA must therefore balance delegated decision speed with retained governance accountability: approval actions may be delegated, but ultimate governance responsibility must remain traceable.[citation:7][citation:8]

For HR data and system access, HR DOA must separate “system capability” from “management authority.” The EU GDPR and ICO employment-data guidance emphasise lawful processing, transparency, documentation, and controller/processor responsibility. HR DOA must therefore cover employee-data changes, recruitment data processing, HRIS access, and third-party HR service providers within the approval and audit framework.[citation:9][citation:10][citation:11][citation:12]

This report provides three outputs: first, an HR DOA Management Policy; second, an HR DOA Approval and Control Standard; and third, templates for authority matrices, exceptions, decision records, violation classification, and monitoring indicators. Specific monetary thresholds, job grades, title names, and country-level legal procedures should be calibrated by each organisation based on geography, industry, organisational scale, and the existing Finance DOA.

### 1.2 Policy Principles

HR DOA should follow these principles:

1. **Legality first**: no internal authority may override local employment law, data protection law, works council or union procedures, mandatory filings, or regulatory requirements.
2. **Authority must match accountability**: approval authority may be delegated, but supervision, compliance accountability, and ultimate governance responsibility must remain traceable.
3. **Segregation of duties**: request, business approval, HR professional review, budget confirmation, legal/compliance review, system execution, and audit testing should not be performed by the same unrestricted role.
4. **Risk tiering before monetary thresholds**: low-cost HR decisions may still create high employment-dispute, discrimination, privacy, reputational, or organisational-stability risk.[citation:1]
5. **System access is not approval authority**: a user’s ability to create, change, or release data in HRIS does not mean that the user is authorised to approve the underlying HR decision.[citation:1][citation:10]
6. **Recordkeeping is control**: every material HR decision must answer who requested it, who reviewed it, who approved it, the basis for approval, when it was approved, whether an exception existed, and whether subsequent changes occurred.[citation:1][citation:11]
7. **Global consistency with local calibration**: global rules should standardise principles, risk language, and control logic; local rules should calibrate approval levels, labour-law procedures, employee-representative mechanisms, and thresholds.

## 2. HR DOA Management Policy

### 2.1 Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish a consistent, explainable, and auditable HR decision-authority framework that:

- defines approval, review, recommendation, veto, execution, and information rights for HR matters;
- prevents unauthorised approval, off-system commitments, retroactive approval, threshold splitting, and conflicts of interest;
- supports global consistency, local employment-law compliance, employee fairness, and organisational efficiency;
- provides the foundation for HRIS configuration, audit sampling, compliance investigations, and management accountability.

### 2.2 Scope

This policy applies to all decisions affecting employee status, roles, compensation, performance, discipline, termination, organisational structure, HR data, and external HR services. It covers:

- permanent employees, fixed-term employees, part-time employees, temporary workers, agency workers, outsourced workers, interns, consultants, executives, and key-position holders;
- candidates, former employees, and rehire cases;
- global headquarters, regional headquarters, country entities, business units, shared service centres, HR COEs, HRBPs, Finance, Legal, Compliance, Privacy, Internal Audit, and external HR service providers.

### 2.3 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| HR DOA | The rules governing delegation, approval, review, veto, recordkeeping, and oversight of HR decisions. |
| Approval | The authority to give internal organisational approval for an HR decision. |
| Review / Confirmation | Professional review of compliance, budget, compensation, legal, employee-relations, or data risk. |
| Veto | Authority to stop a decision where legal, compliance, privacy, employee-relations, or material risk requirements are not met. |
| Recommendation | A business or professional recommendation that does not itself approve the action. |
| Information Right | The right to be informed before or after a decision; this is not approval. |
| Exception | A matter that deviates from standard policy, budget, pay range, headcount plan, labour-law procedure, or system rule. |
| Temporary Delegation / Proxy Approval | Limited authority exercised by a written substitute when the formal approver is absent or a special project requires it. |
| Off-System Commitment | An oral, email, instant-message, or informal commitment concerning offer terms, pay, role, bonus, or termination that has not passed DOA. |

### 2.4 Governance Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Main Responsibility | Authority Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Board / CEO / Executive Committee | Approve overall HR DOA principles and highest-risk matters | Does not manage daily approvals, but retains ultimate governance responsibility for major organisation, reward, and executive matters |
| CHRO / Global HR | Own the global HR DOA framework, interpret principles, approve major exceptions | Does not replace local legal judgment |
| Regional HR | Translate global rules into regional control standards | May not lower global minimum controls |
| Country HR / HRBP | Execute local HR DOA and ensure employment-law and employee-relations compliance | May not independently approve matters requiring Legal, Finance, or business review |
| Business Leader | Own workforce need, business rationale, performance facts, and organisational impact | Business consent does not equal HR compliance approval |
| Finance | Confirm budget, cost, accrual, and financial impact | Budget approval does not mean the HR decision is lawful or fair |
| Legal / Compliance / Privacy | Review or veto employment-law, compliance, data-protection, investigation, and dispute risk | Legal advice does not equal business approval |
| HR Operations / HRIS | Execute system entries, data changes, and record archiving after valid approval | System execution does not equal management approval |
| Internal Audit | Independently test design and operating effectiveness of HR DOA | Does not participate in first-line approval |

This structure aligns with the Three Lines logic: business and HR operations own first-line controls; HR COEs, Legal, Compliance, Privacy, and Finance provide second-line oversight and expert review; Internal Audit provides third-line independent assurance.[citation:3]

### 2.5 Authority Lifecycle

Each delegated authority must specify:

1. source of authority: position, organisational level, committee appointment, written delegation, or system role;
2. delegate: individual, role, committee, or functional office;
3. scope: matter type, monetary threshold, employee type, country/region, business unit, and risk level;
4. effective conditions: appointment date, system configuration, training completion, and conflict-of-interest disclosure;
5. expiry conditions: termination, transfer, reorganisation, project completion, expiry date, or cancellation due to violation;
6. proxy arrangement: proxy approver, period, permitted matters, and prohibited matters;
7. review cycle: at least annual review; semi-annual review for high-risk countries or business units.

## 3. HR DOA Scope and Risk Tiering

### 3.1 HR Matters That Must Be Covered

| Module | Typical Matters | Key Risks |
|---|---|---|
| Workforce planning and organisation design | New headcount, position creation, organisation redesign, reporting-line change | Cost leakage, role conflict, unclear governance |
| Recruitment and hiring | Hiring approval, candidate screening, offer, employment contract, background checks | Discrimination, privacy, pay equity, off-system commitments |
| Compensation and incentives | Starting salary, salary increase, bonus, long-term incentives, allowances, special payments | Budget, tax, fairness, regulatory, reputational risk |
| Promotion and transfer | Promotion, demotion, transfer, assignment, key-position appointment | Fairness, conflict of interest, data access, contract change |
| Performance and discipline | Performance rating, improvement plan, investigation, warning, grievance | Procedural fairness, weak evidence, retaliation or discrimination |
| Separation and redundancy | Resignation, dismissal, redundancy, settlement, severance, rehire | Employment dispute, legal procedure, union/employee representative, reputation |
| Contingent workforce | Agency labour, outsourcing, consultants, interns, independent contractors | Misclassification, joint-employer, data processing, cost control |
| HR data and systems | Employee master data, payroll data, contract data, HRIS access, data export | Privacy, accuracy, access control, audit trail |
| HR vendors | Recruiters, payroll vendor, background-screening provider, EOR, HRIS vendor | Processor responsibility, cross-border transfer, confidentiality, service quality |

### 3.2 Risk Levels

| Level | Definition | Examples | Minimum Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Low | Routine, low-cost, low employee impact, within standard policy | Standard training registration, non-sensitive profile update | First-line manager or HR Ops review; system record |
| L2 Medium | Involves pay, role, contract, or limited budget impact | Standard-range hiring, normal pay increase, internal transfer | Business owner + HRBP + Finance/COE review where relevant |
| L3 High | Above budget, outside pay range, key role, multi-employee impact, or local legal procedure | Exceptional hiring, special bonus, disciplinary action, key-role transfer | Country HR + Legal/Compliance/Finance + regional approval |
| L4 Material | Organisation design, redundancy, collective employee relations, executives, or material reputation risk | Redundancy programme, major restructuring, executive appointment or dismissal, settlement | Regional/Global HR + Legal + Finance + CHRO/CEO-level approval |
| L5 Prohibited / Reserved | Legally prohibited, ethically conflicted, or reserved to board / committee | Undisclosed related-party hiring, avoidance of redundancy procedure, major misconduct-related action | Prohibit or submit to board / dedicated committee |

### 3.3 Risk Dimensions

HR DOA should not rely only on monetary limits. It should combine at least the following dimensions:

- monetary value: annualised cost, one-off payment, severance, bonus, vendor spend;
- headcount: number of affected employees, team size, group impact;
- employee type: executive, key role, sensitive role, temporary worker, agency worker, outsourced worker;
- legal factors: labour law, unions, works councils, anti-discrimination, dismissal protection, cross-border assignment;
- data factors: sensitive personal data, compensation data, health data, disciplinary records, cross-border data;
- fairness: pay equity, promotion consistency, disciplinary consistency, severance consistency;
- organisation factors: organisation design, reporting line, segregation of duties, business continuity;
- reputational factors: media, regulator, employee complaints, social-media impact.

## 4. Approval Matrix Standard

### 4.1 Authority Types

All HR DOA matrices should use a standard rights taxonomy:

| Code | Meaning | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| R | Request | Initiates the matter and provides facts, rationale, and documents |
| REC | Recommend | Provides business or professional recommendation; does not approve |
| REV | Review | Reviews policy, budget, legal, data, employee-relations, or other risk |
| A | Approve | Gives final internal approval |
| V | Veto | Stops the matter where high-risk requirements are not met |
| I | Inform | Is informed; this is not review or approval |
| E | Execute | Executes system or document action after valid approval |

### 4.2 Standard Matrix Template

| Matter | Risk Trigger | Request | Review | Approve | Veto | Execute | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New headcount | New HC or above budget | Business | HRBP, Finance | Country/Regional Business + HR | Finance/CHRO over threshold | HRIS | HC request, budget approval |
| Offer issuance | Within standard pay range | Hiring Manager | HRBP, Compensation if needed | Business Leader + HR | Legal for non-standard terms | TA/HR Ops | Offer approval, salary benchmark |
| Exceptional hiring | Above range, related party, key role | Hiring Manager | HRBP, Compensation, Legal, Compliance | Country HR + Regional HR | Legal/Compliance | TA/HR Ops | Exception memo, COI disclosure |
| Salary change | Standard cycle | Manager | HRBP, Compensation, Finance | Business + HR | Compensation/Finance if budget breach | HRIS/Payroll | Salary change record |
| Special bonus | Non-standard or above budget | Business | HRBP, Compensation, Finance | Regional HR/CHRO | Finance/Compliance | Payroll | Bonus rationale, approval chain |
| Disciplinary action | Written warning or above | Manager/ER | HRBP, ER, Legal | Country HR + Business | Legal/ER | HR Ops | Investigation and hearing records |
| Dismissal / redundancy | Involuntary termination | Business/HR | ER, Legal, Finance | Country/Regional HR + Business | Legal | HR Ops/Payroll | Legal memo, severance calculation |
| Employee data change | Pay, contract, discipline, identity data | HR Ops/HRBP | Data Owner, Privacy if sensitive | HR Data Owner | Privacy/Compliance | HRIS | Ticket, audit log, approval |
| HRIS access | Sensitive data access | Manager | HRIS, Data Owner, Privacy | System Owner + HR Owner | Privacy/Security | IAM/HRIS | Access request, role mapping |

### 4.3 Mandatory Escalation Triggers

The following circumstances must trigger higher-level approval or committee review:

1. above budget, above pay range, or above headcount plan;
2. executives, key positions, or sensitive-data positions;
3. employment dispute, complaint, whistleblowing, investigation, dismissal, or redundancy;
4. unions, works councils, government filings, or regulator notification;
5. family, reporting-line, personal, vendor, budget, or other conflict of interest;
6. cross-border assignment, cross-border data transfer, or external processor involvement;
7. off-system commitment, retroactive approval, or missing approval chain;
8. media, reputation, public-figure, or material organisational impact.

## 5. Key Control Standards

### 5.1 Prior Approval

Except for urgent safety, health, or legal-response situations, all HR DOA matters must be approved before any employee commitment, employee notification, document signing, system change, or payment. Retroactive approval may be used only as a remediation record and must not automatically legitimise the original unauthorised action.

### 5.2 No Splitting to Avoid Thresholds

The organisation must not split value, headcount, timing, organisational unit, or vendor contract to avoid approval thresholds. Audit should aggregate related matters with the same business purpose, time window, employee population, or supplier.

### 5.3 Exception Approval

Every exception must be separately recorded and include at least:

- exception type;
- standard rule;
- deviation;
- business rationale;
- risk assessment;
- compensating controls;
- validity period;
- precedent status;
- final approver;
- Legal, Compliance, Privacy, or Finance review status.

### 5.4 Conflicts of Interest

Approvers must recuse themselves where they have actual or potential family, personal, financial, performance, budget, reporting-line, supplier, or political interest in the employee, candidate, supplier, or outcome. The G20/OECD Principles require board members and key executives to disclose material interests in transactions or matters; HR DOA should extend this logic to hiring, reward, promotion, discipline, termination, and supplier selection.[citation:7]

### 5.5 Local Law Priority

Where global HR DOA conflicts with local employment law, collective agreements, works council processes, data protection law, or regulatory requirements, the stricter or mandatory local requirement prevails. Global DOA must never be used to bypass local statutory procedure.

### 5.6 Employee Data and Privacy Controls

HR data processing must observe lawfulness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, and accountability. The EU GDPR is the central EU framework for personal data protection; ICO employment guidance states that data protection law applies whenever employers process workers’ personal information and covers employees, contractors, volunteers, platform workers, and similar work relationships.[citation:9][citation:10]

### 5.7 Controller, Processor, and Vendor Responsibility

For recruitment, background checks, payroll outsourcing, EOR, HRIS, employee surveys, and other HR-vendor scenarios, the organisation must define whether each party is a controller, joint controller, or processor. ICO recruitment guidance requires organisations to assess and document each party’s status and the personal information collected, used, and stored; written contracts are required for processors, and sub-processors require prior written authorisation.[citation:12]

### 5.8 Discipline and Grievance Procedures

Discipline, grievance, investigation, suspension, dismissal, and related high-risk employee-relations matters must follow fair procedures. ACAS states that employers must follow the Code of Practice when handling disciplinary and grievance issues, and that the procedure and employee actions may be considered by an employment tribunal. HR DOA approvals for discipline and dismissal must therefore review facts, process, evidence, and the employee’s opportunity to respond.[citation:13][citation:14]

## 6. Employee Lifecycle DOA Standards

### 6.1 Workforce Planning and Position Creation

**Applicable matters**: new headcount, replacement hiring, position creation, position elimination, reporting-line change, restructuring.  
**Minimum requirements**: business need, budget source, job grade, organisational impact, local labour or union impact, system position code.  
**Key control**: recruitment must not be opened without approved headcount; job grade and pay range must be reviewed by HR COE / Compensation.

### 6.2 Recruitment and Hiring

**Applicable matters**: hiring launch, candidate screening, interview, background check, offer, employment contract, pre-employment data processing.  
**Minimum requirements**: approval chain, pay range, candidate-data processing status, legality of screening checks, non-discrimination standard.  
**Key control**: oral or email offers must be supported by system or written DOA records; non-standard contract terms require Legal review.

### 6.3 Compensation and Incentives

**Applicable matters**: starting salary, annual increase, special increase, bonus, long-term incentive, allowance, retention bonus, severance.  
**Minimum requirements**: budget, pay range, market benchmark, internal equity, tax/social-security impact, affected employee group.  
**Key control**: business leaders may not decide pay alone; Compensation and Finance separately review pay equity and budget impact.

### 6.4 Promotion, Transfer, and Key Positions

**Applicable matters**: promotion, demotion, transfer, assignment, key-position appointment, sensitive-data-role appointment.  
**Minimum requirements**: role qualifications, performance basis, pay impact, conflict-of-interest disclosure, system-access adjustment, local contract-change requirements.  
**Key control**: role changes involving sensitive data, financial authority, or employee-relations impact must trigger HRIS and access-rights updates.

### 6.5 Performance, Discipline, and Employee Relations

**Applicable matters**: performance-rating calibration, performance improvement plan, investigation, disciplinary action, grievance, suspension, warning.  
**Minimum requirements**: factual record, investigation plan, employee response opportunity, consistency check, Legal/ER review.  
**Key control**: conflicted managers must not approve disciplinary action; material discipline and dismissal require Legal/ER review.

### 6.6 Separation, Dismissal, and Redundancy

**Applicable matters**: resignation, mutual separation, dismissal, redundancy, severance, restrictive covenant, rehire.  
**Minimum requirements**: legal basis, procedural requirements, works council/union requirements, severance calculation, data and system closure, knowledge transfer.  
**Key control**: redundancy and collective-impact matters must be escalated to regional/global level and must not be communicated as final before statutory procedures are completed.

### 6.7 HR Data and Systems

**Applicable matters**: employee master data, payroll data, contract data, performance data, disciplinary records, health data, access provisioning, data export.  
**Minimum requirements**: data-owner approval, purpose statement, least privilege, retention period, audit log, privacy review.  
**Key control**: sensitive-data access must be periodically reviewed; termination, transfer, leave, and proxy approval must trigger access revalidation.

## 7. Violations, Accountability, and Monitoring

### 7.1 HR DOA Violation Types

| Type | Example | Handling Principle |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorised approval | Offer or severance promised without required approval level | Suspend execution, escalate review, record violation |
| Retroactive approval | Employee is informed or payment is made before approval | Treat as exception and violation; not automatically compliant |
| Splitting to avoid thresholds | Splitting pay increases, bonuses, redundancy numbers, or vendor fees | Aggregate and reapprove |
| Off-system commitment | Oral promise of pay, role, bonus, or termination arrangement | Investigate facts and correct or remediate as needed |
| Conflict of interest | Approval of a relative, direct personal-interest case, or related vendor | Void approval and appoint independent approver |
| Missing record | No rationale, review, approval chain, or attachment | Do not execute until evidence is complete |
| System-rights abuse | Unauthorised changes to pay, role, or termination reason | Freeze access and investigate immediately |

### 7.2 Monitoring Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Warning Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Exception rate | Exceptions / total approvals | Country or business unit persistently above average |
| Retroactive approval rate | Retroactive approvals / total approvals | Process bypass or system usability issue |
| Approval cycle time | Time from submission to final approval | Too slow affects efficiency; too fast may indicate missing review |
| Unauthorised-approval count | Number of matters outside DOA | Requires accountability and training |
| System-access anomaly | System rights inconsistent with role or authority | High privacy and fraud risk |
| Dispute linkage rate | Employment disputes involving DOA defects | Indicates controls are not preventing risk |
| Repeat audit finding rate | Recurrence of same DOA issue | Indicates weak remediation |

### 7.3 Review Mechanism

- **Quarterly**: HR Ops / HRIS produces approval, exception, retroactive approval, and access-anomaly reports.
- **Semi-annually**: Country HR with Legal/Compliance reviews local legal changes, works council/union requirements, and high-risk matters.
- **Annually**: Global HR updates the DOA matrix, thresholds, system roles, and training materials.
- **Event-driven**: M&A, restructuring, redundancy, HRIS migration, or shared-service changes trigger immediate review of authority boundaries.

## 9. Conclusion

The core value of HR DOA is to transform people decisions from experience-based approval into an explainable, traceable, auditable, and locally compliant governance mechanism. Effective HR DOA should neither pursue fewer approvals at all costs nor simply add more control layers. It should build a stable structure that balances delegation, professional review, risk escalation, data protection, and accountability.

This report recommends a three-layer HR DOA architecture: first, globally consistent principles and authority definitions; second, risk-tiered and lifecycle-based approval matrices calibrated by local law; third, system access, evidence retention, exception management, monitoring indicators, and audit review. Only when all three layers exist can HR DOA become a common foundation for global HR policy, approval workflow, HRIS rights, and audit control.
