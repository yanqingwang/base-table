## 1. Books

### 1.1 Authenticity: Did Karpathy Write a Book?

**Finding: There is no authoritative book authored by Andrej Karpathy.**

- [1.1.1] ★★★ A self-published book titled *Machine Learning Projects with Python* (ISBN 9798756000665, 132 pages, Oct 2021) lists "Andrej Karpathy" as author. However, the content — basic ML exercises (Iris, Boston Housing, Wine Quality) — is radically inconsistent with Karpathy's expertise level and writing style. **This is likely a fraudulent/name-squatting publication.** Karpathy has never promoted or referenced this book.  
  - Source: [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.sg/Machine-learning-projects-Python-Karpathy/dp/B09KNGGM8H)  
  - Source: [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Machine_Learning_Projects_with_Python.html?id=hxa-zgEACAAJ)

- [1.1.2] ★☆☆ A product page for *The Hundred-Page Language Models Book* claims authorship by "Andrej Karpathy." This likely confuses Karpathy with **Andriy Burkov** (author of *The Hundred-Page Machine Learning Book*). No evidence Karpathy wrote this.  
  - Source: [Expertrainingdownload.com](https://expertrainingdownload.com/product/the-hundred-page-language-models-book/)

- [1.1.3] ★★★ Karpathy's **PhD dissertation** *Connecting Images and Natural Language* (Stanford, 2016, 131 pages) is the closest thing to a book he has authored. Available online as a PDF.  
  - Source: [Stanford Library](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/karpathy/main.pdf) | ★★★

- [1.1.4] ★★★ Karpathy co-authored the **ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge** survey paper (IJCV 2015, ~42 pages), which functions as a definitive reference work.  
  - Source: [IJCV 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-015-0816-y) | ★★★

**Summary: Karpathy has not published a trade book. His written legacy exists in blog posts, his PhD thesis, and research papers. The "Machine Learning Projects with Python" attributed to him is almost certainly fraudulent.**

## 4. Self-Coined Terms & Concepts

### 4.1 Software 2.0 (2017)
- **Definition:** Neural networks as a new programming paradigm where datasets are source code and training is the compiler.
- **First appearance:** [Medium essay](https://karpathy.medium.com/software-2-0-a64152b37c35), Nov 11, 2017
- **Status:** Massively adopted; now a canonical framing.
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary, Karpathy's own essay)

### 4.2 Software 3.0 (2023–2025)
- **Definition:** Prompts/context as programs that program LLMs; English becomes the programming language.
- **First appearance:** YC AI Startup School 2025 talk, expanded in [Sequoia Ascent summary](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/sequoia-ascent-2026/) (2026)
- **Notes:** Karpathy traces early seeds to his 2023 tweet about "the hottest new programming language is English."
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary, Karpathy's own talks/writing)

### 4.3 LLM OS (2023)
- **Definition:** LLM as kernel/CPU, context window as RAM, tools/peripherals as I/O devices — a new operating system architecture.
- **First appearance:** X/Twitter thread, Sep 2023; elaborated in YC talk 2025.
- **Status:** Widely cited metaphor in AI infrastructure.
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary)

### 4.4 Vibe Coding (2025)
- **Definition:** "Fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget that the code even exists" — coding by prompting AI, not writing code.
- **First appearance:** X/Twitter "shower thought," Feb 2025
- **Status:** Went viral; got its own Wikipedia page; YC reported 25% of Winter 2025 batch codebases were 95% AI-generated.
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary)

### 4.5 "We're Summoning Ghosts, Not Building Animals" (2025)
- **Definition:** AI systems are not animal-like robust intelligences; they are "jagged," brittle, ghost-like phenomena trained on human text.
- **First appearance:** *Animals vs Ghosts* essay, Oct 2025
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary)

### 4.6 Space of Minds (2025)
- **Definition:** Animal intelligence is just one point in a vast, mostly-unexplored landscape of possible intelligences; LLMs occupy different points.
- **First appearance:** *The Space of Minds* essay, Nov 2025
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary)

### 4.7 Verifiability as Organizing Principle (2025)
- **Definition:** In the AI era, the key meta-skill is "reset, repeat, score" — creating reliable evaluation loops.
- **First appearance:** *Verifiability* essay, Nov 2025
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary)

### 4.8 Iron Man Suit (2025)
- **Definition:** The most effective human-AI collaboration pattern: human judgment + AI amplification, like Iron Man's suit.
- **First appearance:** YC AI Startup School 2025
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary)

### 4.9 Autonomy Sliders (2025)
- **Definition:** AI systems should have adjustable autonomy levels depending on task risk; not a binary "on/off."
- **First appearance:** YC AI Startup School 2025
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary)

### 4.10 Nano Banana (2025)
- **Definition:** Karpathy's shorthand for the convergence of text generation, image generation, and world knowledge into unified models (like Google's Gemini).
- **First appearance:** *2025 LLM Year in Review*, Dec 2025
- **Source:** ★★★ (primary)

## 5. Core Beliefs

The following theses appear ≥3 times across Karpathy's writing and talks:

### 5.1 "You cannot trust a stack you can't build yourself"
- **Evidence:** This is the driving philosophy behind *Zero to Hero*, *microgpt*, *llm.c*, *nanoGPT*, and LLM101n. He builds everything from scratch, in public.
- **Appears in:** Multiple lectures, blog posts, and the LLM101n syllabus (which quotes Feynman: "What I cannot create, I do not understand").
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.2 Neural networks are a fundamentally new programming paradigm, not just a tool
- **Evidence:** The core argument of *Software 2.0* (2017), reinforced in *Software 3.0* (2025), and multiple talks. He has maintained this frame consistently for 8+ years.
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.3 The dataset is the source code; training is compilation
- **Evidence:** *Software 2.0* essay, multiple talks at Spark+AI Summit 2018, YC 2025. This reframing is the foundation of his Software 2.0 concept.
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.4 LLMs represent a discontinuity, not incremental progress
- **Evidence:** "Software Is Changing (Again)" (YC 2025), *Space of Minds* essay, *Animals vs Ghosts* essay. Consistently argues LLMs are not "better software" but a "new kind of computer."
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.5 AI is simultaneously dumber AND smarter than expected
- **Evidence:** *2025 LLM Year in Review*, multiple X threads, Lex Fridman podcast #333. "LLMs are emerging as a new kind of intelligence, simultaneously a lot smarter than I expected and a lot dumber than I expected."
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.6 Education is his deepest passion
- **Evidence:** CS231n at Stanford (2015–), *Zero to Hero* YouTube series (2022–), Eureka Labs (2024–). He explicitly called Eureka Labs "the culmination of my passion in both AI and education over ~2 decades."
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.7 Technology (especially LLMs) enables asymmetric leverage for individuals
- **Evidence:** *Vibe Coding*, *Power to the People* essay, *Iron Man Suit* concept. Believes LLMs democratize creation capacity.
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.8 Biology/evolution provides the best analogies for AI
- **Evidence:** *Animals vs Ghosts*, *Space of Minds*, Nick Lane book recommendations, constant references to evolution, genes, and natural selection in his writing. He sees evolution as the "original optimizer."
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.9 Code is for understanding, frameworks are for production
- **Evidence:** *microgpt* (pure Python, zero dependencies), llm.c (pure C), *nanoGPT*. He builds minimal implementations to teach understanding, then layer efficiency.
- **Source:** ★★★

### 5.10 We are in the "1960s of AI operating systems"
- **Evidence:** *Software 3.0* talks, YC AI Startup School 2025, Sequoia Ascent 2026. The analogy that LLMs are at a stage comparable to the earliest operating systems (1960s).
- **Source:** ★★★

- **Period:** 2015–present (ongoing course)
- **Significance:** Became Stanford's most popular deep learning course; widely watched online.

## 9. Contradictions & Tensions

The following are genuine contradictions or tensions in Karpathy's corpus. Recorded as found, not reconciled.

### 9.1 "We are really, really far away" (2012) vs. rapid progress embrace (2023–2026)
- **2012:** "A depressing look at the state of Computer Vision Research and AI... we are really, really far away" — [blog post](https://karpathy.github.io/2012/10/22/state-of-computer-vision/)
- **2025:** Enthusiastically describes paradigm shifts, LLMs as new OS, vibe coding; founded Eureka Labs.
- **Resolution (partial):** Karpathy has acknowledged he was wrong about timelines. In 2025 Year in Review, he notes being "mildly surprised" by progress.

### 9.2 Build from scratch vs. use existing tools
- **Belief:** "Code is for understanding" — he builds micrograd, nanoGPT, llm.c to teach deep understanding.
- **Practice:** In production (Tesla, OpenAI), he relied on large-scale frameworks, distributed systems, and existing infrastructure.
- **Tension:** Never explicitly reconciled; likely sees them as complementary (understanding ≠ production).

### 9.3 Education accessibility vs. technical elitism
- **Belief:** "Anyone can learn anything" (Eureka Labs mission); zero-to-hero courses assume only Python basics.
- **Practice:** LLM101n syllabus goes to CUDA, distributed optimization, mixed precision — well beyond "minimal prerequisites."
- **Tension:** Acknowledged implicitly: "this course does not yet exist."

### 9.4 Optimism about AI potential vs. caution about AI risks
- **Early (2012–2015):** Emphasized AI limitations, comfort with slow progress.
- **Middle (2017–2022):** Embraced Software 2.0 paradigm but focused on technical groundedness.
- **Recent (2025–):** "LLMs are simultaneously smarter than expected and dumber than expected." Expresses both excitement (vibe coding, power to people) and skepticism (ghosts vs animals).
- **Tension:** He actively maintains both positions simultaneously, treating it as a feature not a bug.

### 9.5 Prolific open-source educator vs. startup founder
- As an individual, he released everything for free: courses, code, blogs.
- As Eureka Labs CEO, he must build a sustainable business around LLM101n and future courses.
- **Tension:** Not yet resolved; LLM101n repo is archived "until it is ready."

## Appendix A: Writing Style Analysis

Based on corpus analysis across all three blogs and Medium:

- **Voice:** Direct, first-person, conversational but precise. Uses informal metaphors ("you can tell I'm really fun at parties").
- **Structure:** Problem → intuition → code/implementation → reflection.
- **Signature move:** Build-from-scratch demonstrations that reveal surprising simplicity.
- **Influences:** Feynman (clarity, "what I cannot create"), Richard Dawkins (evolutionary framing), Ted Chiang (speculative thinking).
- **Tone evolution:** Early posts (2011–2015) more academic; recent posts (2025–) more opinionated, personal, and philosophical.
