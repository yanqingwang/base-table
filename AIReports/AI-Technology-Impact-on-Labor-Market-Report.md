## Executive Summary

Artificial intelligence is fundamentally reshaping the global labor market landscape. This report provides a comprehensive assessment of AI's impact on various labor segments based on systematic analysis of research from the International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Bank, Goldman Sachs, McKinsey, World Economic Forum (WEF), and other authoritative institutions during 2025-2026, combined with technological evolution trends in enterprise management software markets.

**Core Findings:**

1. **Scale of Impact**: AI could technically automate 25-50% of global working hours, but actual employment impact remains structural and gradual—massive aggregate-level unemployment has not yet materialized.

2. **Impact Distribution**: White-collar positions (especially entry-level cognitive workers) and repetitive manual labor face the greatest impact; high-skill and creative positions remain relatively safe.

3. **Income Polarization**: AI-exposed industries show 16.7% wage growth, significantly above national averages, exhibiting "skill-wage polarization" characteristics.

4. **Enterprise Software**: By 2026, 40% of enterprise applications will embed task-specific AI agents; AI-ERP market is projected to grow from $4.5 billion in 2023 to $46.5 billion by 2033.

5. **Manufacturing and Agriculture**: Approximately 2 million U.S. manufacturing workers face displacement risk; agricultural automation has actually created higher-paying complementary jobs through increased production.

6. **Regional Differences**:
   - China: 54% job displacement risk; government launches "AI+Employment" strategy
   - India: 290,000 AI jobs in 2025, projected 32% growth in 2026
   - Southeast Asia: Accelerated manufacturing transformation, differentiated digital economy strategies

## Chapter 1: Global Labor Market AI Impact Overview

### 1.1 Macro Impact Assessment

#### 1.1.1 Quantitative Predictions vs. Reality Gap

Authoritative research institutions have produced varying quantitative assessments of AI's labor market impact:

| Institution | Automation Prediction | Timeframe | Nature |
|-------------|---------------------|-----------|--------|
| Goldman Sachs | 25% work hours | Near-term | Achievable |
| McKinsey | 50% work hours | Theoretical | Potential |
| Goldman Sachs | 6-7% net job loss | Transition | Actual Impact |
| Yale Budget Lab | No significant aggregate impact | 2022-2025 | Empirical Observation |

**Key Insight**: There is a significant gap between theoretical automation potential and actual employment impact. Empirical data shows that as of late 2025, AI's aggregate-level impact on labor markets remains limited, but **structural changes are occurring**—occupational composition is changing faster than historical averages, and this shift appears in job creation velocity before aggregate unemployment data.

#### 1.1.2 Structural Transformation in Labor Markets

Harvard Business School research shows that since ChatGPT's November 2022 launch, job postings for positions involving substantial structured and repetitive tasks have **declined more than twice as much** as positions with minimal AI exposure. This indicates AI's impact has manifested in **the speed of new job creation** rather than massive layoffs.

IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva stated clearly in January 2026: "AI and digital technologies are transforming today's workplace, even those at the forefront of innovation are not immune to disruption."

### 1.2 Four AI Displacement Mechanisms

McKinsey research identifies four distinct AI labor displacement mechanisms:

1. **Task Automation**: Direct substitution of specific work tasks
2. **Job Redesign with Productivity Lift**: Redefining job functions, improving human efficiency
3. **Net Headcount Reduction**: Direct workforce reduction
4. **Wage Polarization**: High-skilled workers benefit disproportionately more than low-skilled workers

**Current Dominant Mechanism**: Mechanism 2 (job redesign with productivity lift) is becoming the primary form of enterprise AI adoption, characterized by "augmenting human capabilities" rather than "complete replacement."

## Chapter 4: Agricultural Labor Force Transformation

### 4.1 Unique Logic of Agricultural Automation

#### 4.1.1 Labor Supply-Demand Contradiction

Unlike manufacturing, agriculture faces a **structural labor shortage** problem:

- Average age of agricultural workers is high, young labor supply inadequate
- Seasonal employment peaks and valleys are significant
- Dangerous, strenuous working conditions make recruitment difficult
- Tightening immigration policies exacerbate shortages

**Core Logic**: Agricultural automation is not "replacing labor" but **solving labor supply shortages**.

### 4.2 Agricultural AI Application Scenarios

| Application Scenario | Maturity Level | Labor Impact |
|---------------------|---------------|--------------|
| Autonomous Farming Vehicles | Mature (Level 2-3) | Replaces drivers |
| Precision Planting/Fertilization | Mature | Reduces labor demand |
| AI Pest/Disease Identification | Early application | Reduces field inspection |
| Picking Robots | Mid-term (some fruits/vegetables) | Replaces pickers |
| Smart Irrigation/Climate Control | Mature | Reduces human monitoring |

### 4.3 Agricultural Labor Market Empirical Research

#### 4.3.1 Re-examining Labor Impact

Stanford/UC Davis research (Hill, Charlton, Taylor, 2025) provides counter-intuitive findings:

> "Contrary to beliefs that automation reduces employment, we show that automation can raise employment and wages by increasing farm production and creating higher-paid and more desirable jobs in complementary sectors."

**Key Insight**: Agricultural automation's labor impact mechanism differs fundamentally from manufacturing:

1. **Production Effect**: Automation increases agricultural output, expands economic scale, actually increases overall employment
2. **Skill Upgrade Effect**: Low-skill positions are replaced but create higher-paying technical positions
3. **Complementary Effect**: Agricultural automation creates new jobs in equipment operation, maintenance, data analysis

#### 4.3.2 Transformation Challenges

Although long-term net positive employment effects are possible, the transition presents significant pain points:

- **Skills Gap**: Traditional agricultural workers struggle to meet new technical position requirements
- **Regional Imbalance**: Large-scale mechanization progresses quickly in plains, slowly in hilly/mountainous areas
- **Seasonal Characteristics**: Year-round employment imbalance remains unresolved

## Chapter 5: Major Regional Labor Market Analysis

### 5.1 China: 54% Job Displacement Risk and "AI+Employment" Strategy

#### 5.1.1 AI Impact Status

According to Tencent Research Institute's September 2025 survey, approximately **40% of Chinese respondents** expressed concern about AI replacing their jobs. DeepSeek AI startup's senior researcher warned at a November 2025 conference that **most jobs could be eliminated by AI in the next decade**.

Academic research based on LightGBM prediction models shows approximately **54% of jobs in China** could be replaced by AI in coming decades. Relatively speaking, frontline supervisors face the lowest risk, while positions intensive in perceptual and manipulative tasks are most susceptible to replacement.

#### 5.1.2 Policy Response

China has adopted proactive intervention strategies in response to AI employment impact:

- **MOHRSS 2026 AI Employment Policy**: Launch targeted employment support measures
- **"AI+Employment" National Strategy**: Goal to protect 77% of workforce from automation
- **5 Million AI Talent Gap**: Accelerate AI talent cultivation
- **Youth Employment Focus**: Special support for university graduates and young job seekers

#### 5.1.3 Labor Market Characteristics

| Dimension | Current Status |
|-----------|----------------|
| Youth Unemployment Rate | 18.7% |
| Jobs at Risk from AI | 54% |
| Workers Concerned About AI Replacement | 40% |
| AI Talent Gap | 5 million |

#### 5.1.4 Industry Transformation Case Study

**Case: iFlytek Healthcare's "AI Trainer" Transformation**

At iFlytek Healthcare's office in Hefei, Hu Pingping, a former doctor specializing in stomach and gut diseases, now analyzes how medical large language models diagnose complex cases. Having transitioned into an AI trainer five years ago, her new profession merges clinical expertise with cutting-edge AI technology. This shift mirrors a broader professional evolution trend.

#### 5.1.5 China Response Strategy Analysis

China employs a "state-led employment protection" model characterized by:

1. **Priority**: Social stability > Pure efficiency
2. **Corporate Responsibility**: Mandatory corporate participation in retraining
3. **Scale Orientation**: Large-scale talent cultivation programs
4. **Balancing Logic**: Seeking balance between AI productivity gains and 18.7% youth unemployment

- **"Jobless Growth" Risk**: Productivity improvements may lead to "growth without employment"

- **Skills Matching**: Traditional IT talent faces challenges transitioning to AI roles

## Conclusion

### Core Judgments

1. **Not "Job Apocalypse," but "Transformation Period"**: AI's impact on labor markets is structural, not aggregate. Massive unemployment won't occur in the short term, but occupational composition, employment forms, and skill requirements are undergoing profound changes.

2. **Uneven Impact Distribution**: Entry-level positions, medium-low skill groups, and traditional white-collar professions face significant impact; high-skill innovative positions and human-interaction-intensive positions remain relatively safe.

3. **"Augmentation" Trumps "Replacement"**: Successful enterprise practices show that positioning AI as "augmentation" rather than "automation" yields better business and social outcomes.

4. **Policy Choices Are Crucial**: The direction and magnitude of AI's labor market impact depend on educational policy, labor market policy, and social security systems. **The choice lies with humans**.

5. **Significant Regional Differences**:
   - China: 54% job displacement risk, state-led "AI+Employment" strategy
   - India: 290,000 AI job growth, "decoupling" phenomenon emerges
   - Southeast Asia: Manufacturing transformation accelerates, digital economy develops differently
   - Developed economies: Human-machine collaboration dominates, balancing regulation and innovation

### Action Recommendations

**For Enterprises**:

- Establish "AI+Human" workforce planning frameworks
- Invest in employee skills upgrading rather than simple layoffs
- Prioritize "augmentation" AI applications

**For Individuals**:

- Master AI tool usage skills
- Develop "core human capabilities" that AI cannot easily replace
- Build awareness and ability for lifelong learning

**For Policymakers**:

- Establish forward-looking skills prediction and training systems
- Improve social security adapting to new employment patterns
- Support "human-machine collaboration" over pure "robots replace workers" industrial policies
