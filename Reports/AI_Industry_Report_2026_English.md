## III. AI Agents: Core Technology Trend of 2026

### 3.1 Definition and Characteristics of Agents

According to McKinsey's definition, AI agents represent a category of systems built upon generative AI foundation models, capable of autonomous action in real-world scenarios and executing multi-step processes. They typically leverage natural language processing to automate complex tasks previously requiring human effort. Compared to traditional AI assistants, agents possess four core capabilities: **memory** (remembering prior interactions and context), **planning** (breaking complex goals into executable subtestration** (coasks), **orchordinating multiple subtasks and resources), and **integration** (seamlessly connecting with external systems and APIs).

### 3.2 Enterprise Agent Application Status

According to McKinsey's research, over 60% of enterprises are experimenting with AI agents, yet only just over 20% have achieved large-scale agent deployment. By industry, the technology sector leads with 22% of tech companies achieving large-scale AI agent applications. In "The State of AI," McKinsey notes that 88% of companies regularly use AI in at least one business function (up from 78% last year), but only 6% can effectively transform AI investments into profits—revealing the industry's core challenge: "difficult to scale, even harder to monetize."

In application scenarios, AI agents are breaking through first in: **Customer Service and Support** (intelligent customer service robots handling complex inquiries autonomously), **Software Development** (AI coding assistants completing module development tasks independently), **Data Analysis and Insights** (agents autonomously completing data collection, cleaning, analysis, and visualization), **Operational Automation** (agents executing end-to-end business processes independently), and **Content Creation** (agents completing the full content production workflow from planning to publishing).

### 3.3 Challenges and Bottlenecks in Agent Deployment

McKinsey identifies six major challenges in agent deployment: First, **insufficient workflow redesign**—enterprises often focus on technology rather than processes; second, **incomplete data infrastructure**—agents require high-quality, real-time data inputs; third, **lacking organizational capabilities**—shortage of talent combining business and technology expertise; fourth, **lagging governance frameworks**—AI decision transparency and explainability demand new governance mechanisms; fifth, **security and compliance risks**—agent autonomy introduces new security challenges; sixth, **difficulty quantifying ROI**—agent value proves challenging to measure with traditional metrics.

### 6.1 Core Conclusions

First, the global AI industry in 2026 stands at a critical inflection point transitioning from technology validation to large-scale deployment, with market size projected to reach $2.52 trillion and China's market exceeding 1.2 trillion RMB. Second, AI Agents (Agentic AI) have emerged as the core technology trend, with 2026 being designated the "Year of AI Agents." Fifth, AGI timelines are becoming clearer, with the industry generally believing human-level general intelligence will emerge within the next 3-5 years.

### 6.2 Enterprise Recommendations

For enterprise leaders seeking to seize opportunities in this AI wave, we propose the following recommendations:

**Strategic Level**: Elevate AI from "technology projects" to "core strategy," establishing an AI-first organizational culture; monitor AI agent technology trends and proactively build Agent capabilities.

**Execution Level**: Transition from "exploratory pilots" to "scaling and value realization," establishing quantifiable AI value assessment frameworks; emphasize workflow redesign, deeply integrating AI with business processes rather than simply layering AI on top.

**Organizational Level**: Strengthen AI talent development, particularly "bridge-type" talent combining business and technology; establish AI governance frameworks to ensure AI application compliance and security.

**Investment Level**: Continuously increase AI infrastructure investment, but emphasize return on investment; establish diversified AI technology partnership networks, avoiding over-reliance on single vendors.
