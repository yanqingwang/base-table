# Korea Payroll & Attendance HR System Services Market Analysis Report

> Analysis Dimensions: Service Provider Analysis, Buyer Perspective, Multinational Enterprise Analysis, Market Trends & Recommendations

---

## Executive Summary

Korea's Human Resources (HR) system market, particularly in payroll and attendance management, is undergoing a critical period of digital transformation. This report systematically analyzes the current market status of Korea's payroll-attendance HR system services from three dimensions: service provider perspective, buyer (enterprise) perspective, and multinational enterprise practices. It provides decision-making reference for enterprise HR leaders.

**Key Findings:**
- **Market Size**: Korea HR Tech market approximately USD 686 million (2024), projected to reach USD 1.32 billion by 2033 (7.55% CAGR)
- **Payroll & HR Solutions Market**: Approximately USD 1.68 billion by 2031
- **Pricing Range (1,000-employee enterprise)**: KRW 150-500 million per year (USD 105,000-350,000)
- **Per-Employee Cost**: KRW 150,000 per year (USD-500,000 105-350)
- **Market Leaders**: SAP, Workday (enterprise), Hanbiro, Saramin (SMB)
- **Government Subsidy**: Up to KRW 1.8 million per year for companies under 30 employees

## 3. Multinational Enterprise Analysis

### 3.1 HR System Usage Status of Multinational Enterprises in Korea

According to KOTRA (Korea Trade and Investment Promotion Agency), there are approximately **12,205 foreign-invested companies** operating in Korea. The distribution of HR systems among these multinational enterprises varies significantly by country of origin.

#### Main Selection Models

| Model | Percentage | Representative Enterprises |
|-------|------------|---------------------------|
| **Global Unified System** | 50% | Samsung, SK and other Korean global enterprises |
| **Regional Unified + Local Adaptation** | 30% | Foreign-affiliated Korean subsidiaries |
| **Korea Local System** | 20% | SMB |

### 3.2 American Companies in Korea

American companies operating in Korea predominantly use global HR systems to maintain consistency with their headquarters. **Workday** is the most popular choice among American enterprises.

#### Key Customer Cases in Korea

| Company | Industry | System Used | Employee Scale | Implementation Partner |
|---------|----------|-------------|----------------|----------------------|

| **Dow Korea** | Chemicals | Workday | 2,000+ | KPMG |
| **KEB Hana Bank** | Banking | Workday/SAP | 5,000+ | Multiple partners |

#### Workday Market Position in Korea

- **Global**: 50% of Fortune 500 companies use Workday
- **Worldwide Customers**: 9,500+ enterprises
- **Employee Users**: 60 million+ on Workday platform
- **Customer Satisfaction**: 97%

#### Case Study: Korean Air

Korean Air, Korea's flagship carrier, implemented Workday to support its transformation into a global brand. The system enabled:
- Unified HR processes across global operations
- Real-time workforce analytics
- Enhanced employee self-service capabilities
- Integration with existing financial systems

#### Case Study: KEB Hana Bank

KEB Hana Bank, a major Korean commercial bank, uses SAP SuccessFactors for its HR management, demonstrating that some American financial institutions prefer SAP's established banking sector expertise.

### 3.3 Chinese Companies in Korea

Chinese companies in Korea represent a growing segment, though their HR system adoption patterns differ from Western multinationals. The approach typically depends on whether the company is a state-owned enterprise or privately held.

#### System Selection Patterns

| Company Type | Preferred System | Percentage | Notes |
|--------------|-----------------|------------|-------|
| **Large Private Companies (ByteDance, Alibaba, etc.)** | SAP/Workday or Custom | 40% | Prefer global systems for compliance |
| **State-Owned Enterprises** | Local Korean Systems | 35% | Focus on localization |
| **Medium-Sized Companies** | Chinese-developed Systems | 25% | Cost considerations |

#### Key Observations

1. **ByteDance (Korea office)**: Uses a combination of local Korean HR systems and internal Lark (飞书) platform for employee communication
2. **Alibaba (Korea office)**: Utilizes both global SAP systems and regional adaptations
3. **Huawei Korea**: Follows global SAP standards while localizing for Korean regulations
4. **Xiaomi Korea**: Primarily uses local Korean HR solutions due to smaller headcount

#### Chinese Companies' Challenges in Korea

- **Data Localization**: Chinese companies must navigate Korea's strict data protection laws (PIPA)
- **Cross-border Data Transfer**: Restrictions on transferring employee data to China
- **Dual System Operation**: Often maintain separate systems for Korean operations
- **Language Barriers**: Need for bilingual (Korean/Chinese) HR support

### 3.4 European Companies in Korea

European companies in Korea show varied preferences, with **SAP** being the dominant choice due to strong historical ties between European and Korean enterprise systems.

| Country of Origin | Preferred System | Examples |

| **German** | SAP SuccessFactors | Samsung, LG (Korean Chaebols with German roots) |

### 3.6 Multinational Enterprise Focus Points

#### Integration with Global Systems

- Data synchronization with headquarters HR system
- Multi-language, multi-currency support

#### Compliance Requirements

- Korean labor law compliance (overtime, leave, etc.)

- Korean language interface and operation process

## 7. Summary

### 7.1 Key Findings

1. **Market Size**: Korea HR system market approximately KRW 3 trillion, annual growth rate 15-20%
2. **Provider Landscape**: Global providers (SAP, Workday) occupy large enterprises, local providers dominate SMB
3. **Price Range**: Annual cost for 1,000-employee enterprise approximately KRW 150M-500M (USD 105,000-350,000)
4. **Service Model**: SaaS becoming mainstream, remote service proportion continuously increasing
5. **Multinational Enterprises**: Mostly adopt global systems but require local adaptation

### 7.2 Key Success Factors

- **Business-IT Alignment**: Ensure system meets actual business needs
- **Change Management**: Employee training and acceptance determine success/failure
- **Continuous Optimization**: Post-implementation continuous improvement mechanisms
- **Supplier Relationship**: Build long-term partnership

### 7.3 Next Steps

> It is recommended that HR leaders choose priority actions based on actual enterprise circumstances:

| Enterprise Situation | Recommended Action |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Current system has many problems** | Immediately start system evaluation |
| **Facing compliance risks** | Prioritize suppliers with strong compliance |
| **Limited budget** | Evaluate SaaS solutions, utilize government subsidies |
| **Planning globalization** | Choose global providers |
| **Limited personnel** | Consider BPO integration services |
