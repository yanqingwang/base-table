## Executive Summary (fill when writing; must be auditable)

- **US public opinion (non-media evidence) shows a clear anti-escalation / anti-intervention tilt**: Pew finds 61% disapprove of Trump’s handling and 59% say using military force in Iran was the wrong decision; AP-NORC finds 60% say recent military actions “have gone too far”; Ipsos finds 66% prefer ending US involvement quickly and 76% oppose deploying US ground troops into Iran. [citation:20][citation:24][citation:25]
- **French public opinion mixes high concern with low appetite for direct involvement**: Ifop finds 85% say the Iranian regime worries them and 85% fear the conflict will spread, yet only 25% support delivering weapons/equipment alongside the US/Israel and 18% support strikes on Iranian soil. [citation:21]
- **MENA public views are structurally mixed on Iran-related questions**: Arab Barometer (fieldwork in 2025) finds no majority positive evaluation of Khamenei’s foreign policy in any surveyed country, though sizable minorities exist (e.g., Tunisia 49%, Iraq 48%); its 2026-03-31 regional publication frames an “erosion of Western legitimacy” and rising perceived legitimacy of alternative powers including Iran, but quantification should be taken from its linked presentation/data. [citation:22][citation:26]

Explicit rule: this report does **not** treat editorials, TV punditry, or newspaper stances as “public opinion.”

**Audit note on “shift since Feb 28”:** the currently verifiable non-media evidence is mostly single-wave snapshots collected in early–late March. For many countries we lack a strictly comparable pre–Feb-28 baseline, so “direction of change” is marked NA unless backed by comparable pre/post indicators.

## 1. Scope, definitions, and method

### 1.1 Included / excluded evidence

**Included (priority order):**

1. Major pollsters (Pew / YouGov / Gallup / Ipsos, etc.)
2. Regional polling & academic research (e.g., Arab Barometer)
3. Research-grade platform/search summaries (with representativeness caveats)

**Excluded:** news/editorials as public-opinion evidence; unverifiable screenshots/retellings.

### 1.2 Key indicators

- Support/oppose military action
- Support/oppose sanctions
- Preference for ceasefire/diplomacy
- Favorability toward Iran (if directly measured)
- Risk perceptions (energy prices, security spillover, humanitarian concerns)

### 1.3 Stance coding + shift rule

For comparability (but strictly non-media evidence only):

- **+2 Strong support for Iran** (or strong opposition to actions against Iran)
- **+1 Support / sympathy**
- **0 Neutral / mixed**
- **-1 Opposition**
- **-2 Strong opposition**
- **NA Insufficient evidence**

We only label a “shift since Feb 28” when both pre- and post-Feb-28 indicators are verifiable.

## 3. Regional public attitudes (excluding media)

### 3.1 United States

- Core poll indicators (late March):
  - Pew (fieldwork 3/16–3/22; published 3/25): 61% disapprove of Trump’s handling; 59% say the decision to use military force was wrong. [citation:20]
  - AP-NORC (fieldwork 3/19–3/23; published 3/25): 60% say recent actions “have gone too far”; 26% “about right”; 13% “not far enough.” [citation:24]
  - Ipsos KnowledgePanel (fieldwork 3/27–3/29; published 3/31): 66% say the US should end involvement quickly even if not all goals achieved; 76% oppose sending US ground troops into Iran; 86% are concerned about risk to US personnel; 77% about financial costs. [citation:25]
- Segmentation (party/age/region): Pew and AP-NORC both indicate strong partisan splits; we do not add subgroup percentages without reading the topline/subgroup tables directly. [citation:20][citation:24]

### 3.2 Europe (break down UK/FR/DE/ES if possible)

- France (Ifop, fieldwork 3/4–3/5; published 3/6):
  - 85% say the Iranian regime worries them; 85% fear spread across the Middle East; 60% fear it will affect Europe; 54% fear a global dimension. [citation:21]
  - Low support for direct involvement: 25% favor delivering weapons/equipment alongside the US/Israel; 18% support strikes on Iranian soil. [citation:21]
- UK/DE/ES: no verifiable, comparable non-media polling is currently in-ledger (marked NA).

### 3.3 Arab public (tier by data availability)

- Arab Barometer (nationally representative surveys fielded 2025-09..2025-11; blog published 2026-03-03): no majority positive evaluation of Khamenei’s foreign policy in any surveyed country, but sizable minorities exist (Tunisia 49%, Iraq 48%, Palestine 36%, Morocco 35%, Lebanon 29%, Jordan 19%, Syria 3%). This is a proxy for Iran-related leadership/foreign-policy perceptions, not a direct “support the conflict” item. [citation:22]
- Arab Barometer (2026-03-31 regional publication): qualitative framing about Western legitimacy erosion and alternative powers (incl. Iran) gaining perceived legitimacy; we treat this as context unless the linked presentation is extracted quantitatively. [citation:26]

### 3.4 China (only if verifiable public-opinion evidence exists)

- No verifiable, reproducible China public-opinion polling/study evidence is currently included in the ledger (marked NA).

## 5. Iran-relevant implications (evidence-constrained)

- **US:** Non-media polling in late March indicates limited public appetite for escalation and ground involvement, which can constrain sustained escalation narratives domestically. [citation:20][citation:24][citation:25]
- **France:** High concern coexists with low support for direct intervention, implying a ceiling on public backing for deeper involvement even under heightened threat perception. [citation:21]
- **MENA:** Available proxy indicators show mixed attitudes toward Iran-related leadership/legitimacy; conclusions about “support for conflict” should remain cautious without direct conflict questions. [citation:22][citation:26]
